About

An engineer who won't stay in one layer.

I'm in my third year of Computer Engineering at Thapar Institute, with a CGPA of 8.50. I build systems end to end: the database, the API, the pipeline and the model, then the tests that prove they work.

My projects cross the stack on purpose. FoodBridge taught me API design, state machines and assignment algorithms. The cricket lakehouse taught me streaming ingestion, data contracts, and how to evaluate a model honestly. The malaria classifier taught me that a model should be able to show why it decided what it decided. PRAMAAN taught me to let a language model gather evidence without letting it decide.

What connects them: I test what I build, I measure before I claim, and I keep reading an error until I understand it. I also like working with people. I've been my batch's group leader since 2025.

    How I work

    Three commitments I hold myself to, and how to reach me.

    • I ship with tests, or I say it isn't finished.The lakehouse's tests caught a bug that silently deleted every row. Tests are why that never shipped.
    • I report the numbers I measured, including the unflattering ones.My own impact metric loses to wickets-per-ball for ranking bowlers. That result is in the write-up.
    • I own my layer and learn the ones around it.On FoodBridge I owned the backend and matcher, then learned enough about the schema and frontend to keep the contracts between them clean.
